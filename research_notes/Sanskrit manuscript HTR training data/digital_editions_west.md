# Digital editions and library projects outside South Asia: images of Devanagari Sanskrit manuscripts with their own transcriptions

Scope note: I researched this on 2026-11-26/27 with web search and fetch, then cloned the GitHub repositories (tst-project/mss, tst-project/siberupakhyana, wujastyk/sushrutaproject, sarit/SARIT-corpus) and queried others through the GitHub API (erc-dharma/tfd-sanskrit-philology, bodleian/south-asian-mss), counting `<pb>`/`<lb>` elements and script declarations myself. The line and page counts below are my counts from those files. "Diplomatic" means the transcription keeps the scribe's spelling and errors and marks folio and line breaks. Status checks (HTTP 200 unless stated): CUDL HTML pages return 403 to automated clients, but CUDL IIIF manifests return 200. OPenn timed out on one pass but loaded on another. muktabodha.org/digital-library returned 406 to curl. The BL typepad blog returned 403.

## 1. Which projects publish both images and a manuscript-specific transcription, and of which manuscripts?

### Takeaway
Outside South Asia, very few projects pair images of a Devanagari Sanskrit manuscript with a full diplomatic, line-by-line transcription of that same manuscript. The only freely downloadable, line-aligned case found is Charles Li's *Vahnipurāṇa śiber upākhyāna* edition: two British Library India Office Devanagari manuscripts, about 17 pages and 127 lines, with TEI `pb`/`lb` and images on Nakala. Several large libraries do pair images with diplomatic *excerpts*, giving incipit, explicit and colophon with folio and line references (Cambridge) or folio references only (Bodleian, NGMCP). Most other projects either have images with no transcription (Penn, Wellcome, Berlin, Tokyo, Leiden, BL), or transcriptions of non-Devanagari manuscripts (Suśruta Project main witnesses, DHARMA, SARIT manuscript links), or Devanagari diplomatic texts whose images are restricted (Documenta Nepalica, which is also mostly Nepali-language).

### Cited Findings

**A. Full diplomatic transcription + images of the same Devanagari manuscript (best fit)**

- **Charles Li, *King Śibi in the Vahnipurāṇa* (TST project, CNRS/Hamburg).** This open-source edition "includes diplomatic transcriptions of all witnesses", and the transcriptions are linked to images of the source material where available. Archived on Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.7446515. Article: *Asian Literature and Translation* 10(1), 2023, doi:10.18573/alt.58 — [tst-project.github.io/siberupakhyana](https://tst-project.github.io/siberupakhyana); [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/7446515); [GitHub repo](https://github.com/tst-project/siberupakhyana)
  - Witness files in the repo: `Vahnipurana/VpA_IO_SAN_1425.xml` and `VpB_IO_SAN_1001.xml` (both declare `script="devanagari"`), plus `VpE_BORI_Limaye_408.xml` (Devanagari, held in India, empty `<facsimile/>`). My counts: VpA has 8 `pb` and 60 `lb`; VpB has 9 `pb` and 67 `lb`; VpE has 5 `pb` and 44 `lb`. The text is in IAST romanisation, not Devanagari Unicode. Example: `<pb n="343r"/><lb n="8"/>|| || ity ādye vahnipurāṇe ... saṃvat 1646 varṣe` — [repo file](https://github.com/tst-project/siberupakhyana)
  - The images are on Nakala, the French repository. The `<graphic url="https://api.nakala.fr/data/10.34847/nkl.a8fej594/...">` entry in VpA resolves (HTTP 200). Nakala metadata for that dataset: title "British Library IO SAN 1425 Vahnipurāṇa, ff. 146v–149r", created 1589, licence **CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0**, description "Photographed at the British Library.", 7 files — [Nakala API](https://api.nakala.fr/datas/10.34847/nkl.a8fej594). VpB points to the Nakala dataset nkl.cc31b030; I did not check its licence.
  - Repo licence: `LICENSE.md` is **Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 International** — [GitHub](https://github.com/tst-project/siberupakhyana)
  - The BL catalogue describes IO SAN 1001 as a Vahnipurāṇa manuscript written in Benares in 1804, catalogued as digitised. Access to the original is on site in London only — [BL Search Archives](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-003719825)
  - The edition covers one chapter, so each manuscript contributes only a few folios.

- **Suśruta Project (Dominik Wujastyk; edition displayed through Saktumiva).** "Clicking a manuscript siglum in the apparatus opens a window with that witness's diplomatic transcription at that point in the text, alongside the matching manuscript image." Witnesses: K = Kathmandu Kaiser Library 699; N = Kathmandu National Archives 1-1079; H = NAK 5-333. The site is "Distributed under a Creative Commons BY-SA 4.0 licence" — [Suśruta edition](https://sushrutaproject1.github.io/Sushrutasamhita_Saktumiva2/)
  - Repo licence: all files "copyrighted (C) Dominik Wujastyk 2021 and distributed under a Creative Commons BY-SA licence" — [wujastyk/sushrutaproject](https://github.com/wujastyk/sushrutaproject)
  - **Script check (my grep of the repo):** the KL 699 and NAK files declare `handNote ... script="newa"`, so they are **Nepalese/Newar script, NOT Devanagari**. The single Devanagari file is `dalhana-nibandhasangraha/bl_iolr_1842_sutrasthana.xml`, "British Library IOLR Taylor 1842 (Eggeling 2646). A manuscript of the Suśrutasaṃhitā with Ḍalhaṇa's commentary, Nibandhasaṅgraha", with 18 `pb` (from f. 140v) and 157 `lb`. That file contains no `<graphic>`/`facs` link, so I found no published image source for it — [GitHub](https://github.com/wujastyk/sushrutaproject)
  - Largest Newar-script files, for comparison: `kl_699_uttaratantra.xml` has 109 `pb` and 720 `lb`; `nak_5-333_uttaratantra-39-24-57.xml` has 85 `pb` and 445 `lb`.

- **Saktumiva platform (Charles Li).** Users "produce transcriptions of documents" and "automatically collate them". The only hosted demonstration is the *Dravyasamuddeśa of Bhartṛhari*. Code: github.com/chchch/upama and github.com/chchch/saktumiva ("collate and analyze multi-witness texts in Sanskrit, Tamil, & Tibetan") — [saktumiva.org](https://saktumiva.org/); [chchch/saktumiva](https://github.com/chchch/saktumiva)
  - The requester's own Manuscribe tool "follows the Saktumiva TEI guide" and exports TEI per folio — [Manuscribe](https://github.com/drdhaval2785/manuscribe). Saktumiva-style TEI (Suśruta, Vahnipurāṇa) is therefore structurally close to the requester's format.

**B. Paratext or excerpt transcriptions linked to images**

- **TST "Texts Surrounding Texts" (ANR/DFG FRAL 2018, 2019–2023; BnF + Staats- und Universitätsbibliothek Hamburg).** The project covers "Satellite stanzas, prefaces, & colophons in South Indian manuscripts (BnF & Hamburg Stabi collections)" — [TST](https://tst-project.github.io/)
  - My survey of the 825 TEI files in [tst-project/mss](https://github.com/tst-project/mss) (licence: Creative Commons Attribution Share Alike 4.0 International): script attributes count tamil 862, grantha 60, telugu 25, nandinagari 24, **devanagari 22** (+3 mixed), bengali 20, newa 3, sarada 2. 241 `mainLang="san"`.
  - About 26 files contain Devanagari hands (e.g. BnF Sanscrit_0053, _1129, _1145, _1752, Indien_0672, _0703, NAF_8895). Their transcribed paratexts carry about 340 `lb` in total (per file 0–174; NAF_8895 alone has 174), with `facs` page pointers to the BnF digitisations. These are colophons and headers, not full texts, and not every Devanagari file is in Sanskrit.

- **Cambridge Digital Library (CUDL) – Sanskrit Manuscripts (AHRC-funded Sanskrit Manuscripts Project).** Catalogue records include an **"Excerpts"** field with diplomatic transcriptions of rubric, incipit, explicit, final rubric and colophon, each tagged with folio and line. Example, MS Add.2432 *Śṛṅgāratilakaṭīkā*: 10 ff., Script "Devanāgarī in black ink", dated "1901 Vikrama / 1844 CE". Excerpt sample: "[1v4] bāhu dvau ca \ mṛ / ṇālamāsyakamalaṃ ... śṛoṇītīrtha[1v5]śilā ... śaro (!) nirmita_ (!)"; the excerpts use editorial signs (!), 〚 〛 (deletions) and \ / (insertions). Licence statement in the manifest: "Images made available for download are licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 Unported License (CC BY-NC 4.0)", and the metadata is under the same licence. 20 canvases; image size 7921×4969 px (JPEG2000 via IIIF) — [CUDL IIIF manifest MS-ADD-02432](https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/iiif/MS-ADD-02432)
  - The project's transcription conventions are deposited in Apollo ("Sanskrit Manuscripts Project: Transcription Conventions", licence "Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 International") — [Apollo 1810/326907](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/326907)
  - Collection portal: [cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/collections/sanskrit](https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/collections/sanskrit) (returns 403 to scripts; works in a browser). A project article describes the aim as a complete electronic catalogue of the Library's Sanskrit/South Asian manuscripts, with about a third of them digitised — [OAPEN volume](https://library.oapen.org/bitstream/20.500.12657/59519/1/9783110543100.pdf)
  - Jain items are present, e.g. MS Add.2313 is tagged Sanskrit, Jaina, Vyākaraṇa — [CUDL IIIF MS-ADD-02313](https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/iiif/MS-ADD-02313)
  - No full-text transcriptions (no IIIF `otherContent`/annotation lists) were present in the MS Add.2432 manifest (my check).

- **Bodleian Libraries – South Asian manuscripts catalogue and Digital Bodleian.** The TEI catalogue sources are on GitHub as `bodleian/south-asian-mss` ("TEI For South Asian Catalogue (WIP)", 3,025 collection XML files, **no licence file**). Entries such as MS. Chandra Shum Shere c.191 declare `script="Devanāgarī"`. They give `<incipit>`/`<explicit>` in IAST with a folio `<locus>` but no line number, e.g. "F. 3v (f. 1 in original foliation): dehārdhayogaḥ śivayoḥ". They link to the images through `<surrogates><bibl type="digital-facsimile" subtype="full"><ref target="https://digital.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/objects/28d9712c-...">`. c.479 links per-page `surfaces/` URLs — [GitHub bodleian/south-asian-mss](https://github.com/bodleian/south-asian-mss); [catalogue](https://south-asian.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/about)
  - Digital Bodleian terms point to CC BY-NC 4.0 and restrict commercial use. Full-resolution JPEG/PDF downloads are offered. "All Digital Bodleian objects have a corresponding IIIF manifest". The IIIF page asks users not to download and re-host the images — [Terms](https://digital.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/terms/); [IIIF API](https://digital.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/developer/iiif); [FAQ](https://digital.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/faq)
  - Collection: [South Asian Manuscripts](https://digital.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/collections/south-asian/). The Clay Sanskrit Library-funded project digitised 100 Sanskrit/South Asian manuscript works and completed in October 2025 — [Clay Sanskrit Library](https://claysanskritlibrary.org/digital-bodleian-platform); [Digital Bodleian Clay collection](https://digital.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/collections/sanskrit-manuscripts-clay/)

- **NGMCP (Nepalese-German Manuscript Cataloguing Project, Universität Hamburg).** The descriptive-catalogue wiki is archived, and Tyler Neill's GitHub Pages mirror lists about 15,833 entry pages. Wujastyk describes it as "descriptive information on nearly 16,000 manuscripts" — [mirror](https://tylergneill.github.io/ngmcp-wiki-mirror/); [Hamburg archive](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/ngmcp/ngmcp.html); [INDOLOGY thread](https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/2025-July/060826.html)
  - Each entry has a Script field and romanised "Excerpts" (Beginning, Sub-Colophons, End, Colophon) in near-diplomatic form: errors are marked "(!)" and lacunae "++ .. ..", with folio references like "(fol. 1v)" but no line numbers. The sample A 1-1 *Abdaprabodha* is **Newari script**, palm-leaf, 80 folios, 6 lines per folio — [entry](https://tylergneill.github.io/ngmcp-wiki-mirror/A%201-1%20Abdaprabodha.html)
  - I found no source saying that NGMCP entries include page images. The NGMPP microfilms are not online in this catalogue — [search summary of Hamburg pages](https://www.aai.uni-hamburg.de/en/forschung/ngmcp/about.html)

**C. Diplomatic Devanagari transcriptions with restricted images; documents, mostly not Sanskrit**

- **Documenta Nepalica (Heidelberg Academy of Sciences and Humanities, "Documents on the History of Religion and Law of Pre-modern Nepal").** Editions are TEI XML. The "Diplomatic" mode preserves "original line breaks", and the viewer offers "Devanagari script or transliteration into Latin script" — [Documenta Nepalica](https://nepalica.hadw-bw.de/nepal/); [editing principles](https://nepalica.hadw-bw.de/nepalwww/ed_prncpl.html)
  - Licences, quoted: editions, translations and comments "is subject to the license CC BY-SA 4.0". "The copyright for the digital facsimiles remains with the National Archives". "All use of the digital facsimiles requires prior written permission by the National Archives." — [HAdW Nepal page](https://www.hadw-bw.de/en/nepal)
  - Language: parts of the documents are in "khasa-kurā (later known as Nepali)". These are legal and administrative documents, not literary manuscripts, and the language is mostly Nepali, not Sanskrit — [Documenta Nepalica](https://nepalica.hadw-bw.de/nepal/)
  - Related HTR work: Sarawgi, Garces Arias and Zotter, "Digitizing Nepal's Written Heritage: A Comprehensive HTR Pipeline for Old Nepali Manuscripts" (arXiv Dec 2025; ACL 2026). Best CER 4.9%. The evaluation dataset is confidential; code and configurations are released — [arXiv 2512.17111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17111v1); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.671/)

**E. Text corpora: check for single-manuscript transcriptions**

- **SARIT.** 85 TEI files in [sarit/SARIT-corpus](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus). The many `<lb>` elements mark line breaks of *printed editions* (e.g. `<lb ed="s" type="hyphen"/>`), not of manuscripts; no file carries a Devanagari manuscript facsimile (my grep). Two cases point to manuscript transcriptions held outside the corpus: *pramanavarttikalankarabhasya.xml* uses `<pb corresp="pva_msE_diplomatic.xml#...">`, and *ratnakirti-nibandhavali.xml* uses `facs="../work/capv-ngmpp-b21_31-transcript.xml#..."` (an NGMPP B 21/31 transcript). Neither diplomatic file is in the corpus, and I did not verify their scripts.
- **GRETIL (SUB Göttingen).** It invites "a diplomatic transliteration of a manuscript" as a contribution, but the main index (gretil.html) has no texts marked as single-manuscript transcriptions (my grep of the 1 MB index for diplomatic/manuscript/NGMPP) — [GRETIL](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil.html)
- **Muktabodha + IFP (Pondicherry, with EFEO).** The IFP portal lists 2,284 "Transcripts of the Indology Collection". These are recent paper Devanagari transcripts (the 2007 figure was 1,144 transcripts, over 2,000 texts, over 200,000 pages) copied from mostly Grantha originals — [IFP Digital Collections](https://digitalcollections.ifpindia.org); [Hinduism Today](https://www.hinduismtoday.com/hpi/?p=12543). Some were made from eGangotri manuscript images and some from IFP material; a 2023 project is converting 75 Grantha palm-leaf manuscripts into e-texts — [Muktabodha digital library](https://muktabodha.org/digital-library/); [75 Śaiva texts](https://muktabodha.org/?p=6429); [INDOLOGY 2023](https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/2023-May/057716.html)

### Inferences
- The only freely usable images with a line-aligned diplomatic transcription of a Devanagari Sanskrit manuscript are the Vahnipurāṇa BL witnesses (and possibly IOLR Taylor 1842, if its images can be found). The Western "edition" landscape is dominated by critical editions and catalogues, not diplomatic transcriptions.
- Catalogue excerpts (CUDL with folio+line; Bodleian and NGMCP with folio only) are the most scalable source in this group. The excerpts are short (often a line or two each for incipit, explicit and colophon) and frequently begin or end mid-line. Using them means cropping partial lines, and the Bodleian and NGMCP excerpts have no line numbers.
- Every transcription found is in IAST romanisation, not Devanagari Unicode. A deterministic IAST→Devanagari conversion is needed. Editorial signs ((!), 〚 〛, \ /, +++, `_` for virāma) must be mapped to the recogniser's label conventions or stripped.
- Licensing: the best-aligned images (Nakala/BL, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), CUDL (CC BY-NC 4.0) and Bodleian (CC BY-NC 4.0, with a request not to re-host) are all non-commercial. The requester's memory notes say their training contributions are CC BY-SA 4.0. Mixing NC-licensed line crops into a CC BY-SA dataset would be a licence conflict. Penn OPenn (Public Domain Mark), some Wellcome items (CC BY / PDM) and UTokyo (CC BY 4.0) are compatible, but they have no transcriptions.
- The IFP transcripts paired with Muktabodha e-texts could be large, but they are 20th-century Devanagari copies of Grantha originals. Their hands differ from 15th–19th c. Jain paper manuscripts, and each pairing would need checking.

### Gaps
- Not researched for lack of time or sources: the Vienna Pramāṇa / STTAR diplomatic editions (manuscripts from Tibet, generally reported as non-Devanagari; no source collected here), "Beyond Boundaries" (Oxford; only a passing CORDIS mention found, epigraphy), the ERC projects "SHAKTI" and "Kālikula", Hamburg's "Nepālamāhātmya" edition, and Göttingen SUB's own Sanskrit manuscript images.

I found no manuscript-image-plus-transcription output — [CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/article/id/423130-untangling-the-traditions-of-yoga-ayurveda-and-alchemy); [AyurYog publications](https://www.ayuryog.org/publications)

## 2. Per project: manuscripts, script, how diplomatic, break marking, image access, licence, URLs

### Takeaway
Of the sources that are both Devanagari and manuscript-specific, the Vahnipurāṇa TEI (pb + numbered lb, diplomatic, IAST) and the CUDL excerpts ([folio+side+line] markers) give the precise line anchors that line-level ground truth needs. The Bodleian and NGMCP give only folio anchors. Documenta Nepalica keeps original line breaks but restricts its images. Penn, Wellcome, Berlin, Tokyo and Leiden have downloadable or IIIF images with no transcription.

### Inferences
- For the requester's pipeline (segment → predict → correct), the CUDL and Vahnipurāṇa material can be imported directly, because the `[folio side line]` anchors and `lb n=` attributes map onto segmented line images. The Bodleian and NGMCP excerpts would need an alignment step, such as running the recogniser and fuzzy-matching the excerpt against the predicted lines near the folio's first or last lines.
- Penn's public-domain Devanagari manuscripts (18th–19th c., many 8–10 lines per leaf) are well suited to the requester's predict-and-correct workflow even though they have no transcription. That would be new ground truth, though, not reuse of existing text.

### Gaps
- How many CUDL and Bodleian records are both Devanagari and digitised with excerpts: not counted. CUDL HTML is blocked to scripts; a full count would need the CUDL IIIF collection or JSON endpoints, or a scan of the 3,025 Bodleian TEI files for `script="Devanāgarī"` plus `<surrogates>`.
- Licence of Nakala dataset nkl.cc31b030 (IO SAN 1001 images): not checked.
- Image source for BL IOLR Taylor 1842 (Suśruta/Ḍalhaṇa): not found.

## 3. Estimated yield of aligned material

### Takeaway
From Western digital editions, freely available line-aligned Devanagari Sanskrit ground truth is tiny: about 17 pages and about 127 lines (Vahnipurāṇa BL witnesses). Adding the Ḍalhaṇa BL file (images not located) and TST Devanagari paratexts brings it to roughly 600 lines at most. Catalogue excerpts (CUDL, and Bodleian after alignment) could add on the order of a few thousand partial lines, under NC licences. That is useful as a test set or a diversity supplement, not as a primary training corpus.

### Cited Findings
- Vahnipurāṇa: IO SAN 1425 has 8 pb and 60 lb; IO SAN 1001 has 9 pb and 67 lb (my counts) — [repo](https://github.com/tst-project/siberupakhyana)
- Ḍalhaṇa BL IOLR Taylor 1842: 18 pb, 157 lb — [repo](https://github.com/wujastyk/sushrutaproject)
- TST Devanagari-hand files: about 26 files, about 340 lb in total (my sum) — [tst-project/mss](https://github.com/tst-project/mss)
- CUDL MS Add.2432: about 10 excerpt line-anchors (1v1, 1v2, 1v4, 1v5, 2r4, 10v6, 10v7, 10v8, 10v9) in one 10-folio manuscript — [manifest](https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/iiif/MS-ADD-02432)
- NGMCP: about 15,833 entries, each with a few excerpt lines, but no images — [mirror](https://tylergneill.github.io/ngmcp-wiki-mirror/)
- IFP transcripts: 2,284 items, more than 200,000 pages (2007 figure), in 20th-century Devanagari hands — [IFP](https://digitalcollections.ifpindia.org); [Hinduism Today](https://www.hinduismtoday.com/hpi/?p=12543)

### Inferences
- If around 200–400 of CUDL's digitised items are Devanagari, at about 8–10 excerpt lines each, that would give about 2,000–4,000 partial lines (an estimate; the item count is unknown). The Bodleian could give a similar order of magnitude but needs alignment.
- The biggest Western-side opportunity is not existing transcriptions but public-domain or CC BY images: Penn OPenn (PDM), Wellcome (CC BY/PDM) and UTokyo (CC BY 4.0). These could be corrected through the requester's own pipeline and released under CC BY-SA without conflict.

### Gaps
- No reliable counts of Devanagari items per library (CUDL, Bodleian, Wellcome, Berlin, Penn) were obtained.
- No source documents a public release of the Oxford/OCHS 500-page Devanagari ground truth.
