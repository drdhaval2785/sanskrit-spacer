# South Asian sources pairing images of handwritten Devanagari Sanskrit manuscripts with transcriptions of the same manuscripts

Scope: repositories, projects and publishers in India and Nepal (plus the Sanskrit Library and JAINpedia, which the brief named). Research done 26–27 Nov 2026. "Verified" means I fetched the URL or downloaded the file myself on those dates. Where a page would not render (JavaScript-only sites) or refused automated access, the Gaps sections say so.

## Key Question 1: Which sources offer manuscript images and matching transcriptions, and how?

### Takeaway
Only two South Asian sources give the full text of a handwritten Devanagari manuscript whose images can also be had. (1) Muktabodha has about 44 diplomatic e-texts (CC BY-NC 4.0) of manuscripts that eGangotri scanned for archive.org, mostly from the Raghunath Temple library in Jammu. They keep folio-side markers and the manuscript's line breaks, but many of the archive.org scans have since been withdrawn, and I could confirm live images for only 8. (2) Muktabodha also has about 69 e-texts typed from the IFP's 20th-century handwritten Devanagari paper transcripts, with page markers; the page images are CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 and need registration. NGMCP (Nepal) gives semi-diplomatic IAST transcriptions of only the opening, closing and colophon passages, with folio references, for about 15,800 manuscripts, roughly half of them Devanagari; the images have to be bought from the Staatsbibliothek Berlin. No Jain source pairs manuscript images with full transcriptions online. The Jain catalogues (Kobā, L. D. Institute) quote only opening and closing lines.

### Cited Findings

**Muktabodha Indological Research Institute: diplomatic e-texts of eGangotri-scanned Devanagari manuscripts (the closest match to the requirement)**
- Muktabodha's Varanasi office made e-texts "from manuscript images made available to the public by the generosity of the eGangotri organisation", supervised by Dr. Anirban Dash. The first batch was announced in June 2023 with the note "The transcriptions of these manuscripts have not been normalized. Word breaks (or lack of word breaks) is as in the devanagari manuscripts", and "v" is kept where it stands for "b". — [Indology list, 9 Jun 2023](https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/attachments/20230609/0ffe3825/attachment.htm); [Indology list, Jun 2023 thread](https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/2023-June/057803.html)
- On 29–30 Nov 2023, 13 more tantric e-texts were announced as "diplomatic transcriptions of the manuscripts they are transcribed from". Five earlier ones were revised to the same standard: Ajñānadhvāntadīpikā MS 4966, Ānandeśvarapaddhati 5520, Dakṣiṇāmūrtisaṃhitā 1633, Mudrāprakāśa 1406, Navārṇavacaṇḍīpañcāṅga 4818. The new ones are Raghunatha Temple MSS 3887, 4913, 5295, 5605, 4853, 4930, 4972, 4826, 5753, 4894 and 1309, Rashtriya Sanskrit Sansthan MS 72 (Mātṛkātantra), and UPSS MS 1322 (Parāmbārcanacandrikā). Transcribers: Vinay Mishra, Dharmendra Kumar, Sandeep Yadav. — [Indology list, Nov 2023](https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/2023-November/058476.html); [credits correction](https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/attachments/20231130/d67eb05c/attachment.htm)
- **Bulk download (verified 27 Nov 2026):** the whole e-text library is two zip files, one IAST and one Devanagari: [MUKTABODHA-LIBRARY-IAST.zip](https://muktalib7.com/DL_CATALOG_ROOT/MUKTABODHA-LIBRARY-IAST.zip) (HTTP 200, 41.9 MB, last-modified 27 Nov 2024) and [MUKTABODHA-LIBRARY-DEVANAGARI.zip](https://muktalib7.com/DL_CATALOG_ROOT/MUKTABODHA-LIBRARY-DEVANAGARI.zip) (HTTP 200, 48.0 MB). The July 2024 announcement says "There are a total of 487 etexts" and that the bundle omits "the etexts of the joint Muktabodha/IFP project". The IAST zip I downloaded holds 499 files. — [Indology list, 2 Jul 2024](https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/attachments/20240702/54937695/attachment.htm). An individual-file download was added in Nov 2024. — [Indology list, 17 Nov 2024](https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/attachments/20241117/9266d1dd/attachment.htm)
- **Licence, verbatim:** the header of every one of the 499 files in the IAST zip reads "Use of this material (e-texts) is covered by Creative Commons license BY-NC 4.0" (my grep count: 499/499). — [MUKTABODHA-LIBRARY-IAST.zip](https://muktalib7.com/DL_CATALOG_ROOT/MUKTABODHA-LIBRARY-IAST.zip)

The site T&C and the per-file CC licence conflict.
- **What a diplomatic e-text looks like (my inspection of the zip):**
  - Example header for M00624 ānandeśvarapaddhatiḥ: "Manuscript : manuscript 5520 gh Dharmartha Trust at Raghunath Temple, Jammu, JandK … Link to original manuscript on archive.org: https://archive.org/download/AnandeshwarPaddhati…_5520_Alm_25_Shlf_2_Gha_Devanagari - Tantra.pdf … Word divisions and the letters v and b are as in manuscript … Revision 1: Nov. 29, 2023 diplomatic transcription".
  - Folio sides are marked inline, e.g. "u0mnā0 pṛ0 1a)", "pṛ0 2ba)". The text runs on without spaces, as in the manuscript, and lines break mid-word ("…pravakṣyāmiyatmātvaṃparipṛchasi ta / syaśravaṇamātreṇa…"), so the e-text lines appear to follow the manuscript lines (M00640 ūrdhvāmnāya).
  - Source: [MUKTABODHA-LIBRARY-IAST.zip](https://muktalib7.com/DL_CATALOG_ROOT/MUKTABODHA-LIBRARY-IAST.zip)
- **Scale (my count from the zip):**
  - 44 e-texts give a manuscript source in the Raghunath Temple Jammu, UPSS Lucknow, Rashtriya Sanskrit Sansthan Lucknow or Dogra Art Museum collections and carry "pṛ0 Na/b)" folio-side markers. Together they have about 5,800 folio-side markers and about 72,700 non-empty text lines (rough; includes some table-of-contents lines).
  - Median lines per folio side varies from 4 to 36 across texts.
  - Some files labelled "diplomatic" are typed from printed books (e.g. M00660 Īśvarasiddhi "Copied from volume 34 of the K.S.T.S. series"), so check each header.
  - Source: [MUKTABODHA-LIBRARY-IAST.zip](https://muktalib7.com/DL_CATALOG_ROOT/MUKTABODHA-LIBRARY-IAST.zip)

**Muktabodha: IFP/EFEO Devanagari paper transcripts and e-texts made from them**
- The IFP collection in Muktabodha is 1,144 paper transcripts holding over 2,000 mostly Śaiva texts. They are "clearly written in ink on paper in the well-known Devanagari script", copied mostly from Grantha palm-leaf originals, and total about 210,000 pages.
  - Images come as DjVu and PDF. Registration is required ("NEW USERS REGISTER HERE, REQUIRED").
  - The catalogue includes "the quotations of the beginnings and ends of texts".
  - Terms: the images are "licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution-Noncommercial-No Derivative Works 3.0 License", under a 31 Jan 2006 IFP–EFEO–Muktabodha agreement.
  - Source: [Muktabodha IFP access page](https://muktalib7.com/DL_CATALOG_ROOT/IFP_CATALOG/access_page.htm)
- Muktabodha calls them "Devanagari paper transcripts of palm-leaf manuscripts that were commissioned and hand-copied by scholars of the IFP", digitised in 2003. — [Muktabodha: 75 Śaiva texts project](https://muktabodha.org/75-saiva-texts-in-new-project-with-ifp-2/)
- **Matching e-texts (my count from the zip):** 69 e-text headers name an IFP or IFP/EFEO transcript as the source, e.g. "IFP/EFEO transcript T0012" (Candrajñānāgama, M00273) and "E-text created from IFP/EFEO transcript T00370 pages 326 to 400" (Āśaucadīpikā, M00237). The bodies carry page markers matching transcript pages ("p. 327)").
  - In the sample I read, the text lines seem to break where the transcript's lines break, but words are separated by spaces. That is, the text is not a strict diplomatic copy.
  - Source: [MUKTABODHA-LIBRARY-IAST.zip](https://muktalib7.com/DL_CATALOG_ROOT/MUKTABODHA-LIBRARY-IAST.zip)
- The newer joint Muktabodha/IFP project is different. It converts "75 specially-selected Śaiva Siddhanta palm-leaf manuscripts written in the Grantha script" into e-texts, so its sources are not Devanagari. — [Muktabodha: 75 Śaiva texts project](https://muktabodha.org/75-saiva-texts-in-new-project-with-ifp-2/); first ten e-texts: [Muktabodha news](https://muktabodha.org/?p=6778)
- Library overview: "More than 3,000 texts … including over 570 searchable e-texts". The legacy interface is still the only way into the IFP and Gokarna Vedic image collections. — [Muktabodha digital library](https://muktabodha.org/digital-library/); new interface [muktabodha-digital-library.org](https://muktabodha-digital-library.org) (verified, 200); legacy [muktalib7.com](https://muktalib7.com/DL_CATALOG_ROOT/digital_library.htm) (verified, 200)

**NGMPP / NGMCP (Nepal): transcribed opening, closing and colophon passages only**
- The Hamburg wiki data ("a simplified version") covers nearly 16,000 manuscripts. It is mirrored at the [Hamburg web archive](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/ngmcp/index.html) and on [tylergneill.github.io](https://tylergneill.github.io/ngmcp-wiki-mirror) (both verified). The Online Title List covers about 117,406 manuscripts. — [Indology list, Jul 2025](https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/2025-July/060838.html); [title list search](https://catalogue.ngmcp.uni-hamburg.de/content/search/ngmcpdocument.xed)
- **Entry structure (verified):** each entry has a "Scripts:" field and "Excerpts" sections titled "Beginning", "Sub-colophon" and "End", transcribed in IAST. They are semi-diplomatic: scribal errors are flagged "(!)", lost akṣaras are given as "+++", uncertain readings are bracketed "⁅ ⁆", and the folio and exposure are cited, e.g. "(fol. 108r) (exp. 005)". The example, A 10-2 Brahmapurāṇa, is in Newari script on palm leaf. — [NGMCP entry A 10-2](https://tylergneill.github.io/ngmcp-wiki-mirror/A%2010-2%20Brahmapur%C4%81%E1%B9%87a.html)
- **Script share (my random sample of 160 of the 15,832 mirrored entries):** 84 Devanagari (52%), 55 Newari, 3 Maithili, 8 "unknown", the rest mixed or unparsable. 76 of the 160 are Devanagari, Sanskrit and paper. 156 of 160 carry folio or exposure references, and 107 are in the "Digitized manuscripts" category. — [NGMCP wiki mirror index](https://tylergneill.github.io/ngmcp-wiki-mirror). In NGMCP usage "Newari" covers several scripts (Bhujimol, Pracalit, etc.). — [Indology list, Dec 2024](https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/2024-December/059991.html)
- **Getting images:** a December 2024 post describes ordering scans of NGMCP microfilms from the Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin. You enter the microfilm number as the shelfmark and the folio count, choose the fee level "Scans of Microfilms", pay the quoted cost, and download; it takes "usually within days; weeks at maximum". — [Indology list, Dec 2024](https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/2024-December/059992.html); [SBB order form](https://webshop.staatsbibliothek-berlin.de/english/sbb_enquiry/request/) (verified, 200)
  - Hamburg's older access page (last updated 23 Mar 2016) says "we CANNOT accept manuscript copy orders at the NRC in Kathmandu" (from 1 Jan 2014) and that the microfilms "may be consulted" in the Berlin oriental reading room. — [NGMCP: Accessing Microfilm](https://www.aai.uni-hamburg.de/en/forschung/ngmcp/about/access.html)
  - Heidelberg Academy contact for scans: nepal@hadw-bw.de. — [Indology list, Jul 2025](https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/2025-July/060838.html)
- The neighbouring Heidelberg "Documenta Nepalica" platform publishes TEI editions of documents, mostly Nepali, with a diplomatic mode that "reproduces the original document as faithfully as possible" and keeps the line breaks. — [Documenta Nepalica](https://nepalica.hadw-bw.de/nepal/). An Old Nepali Devanagari HTR study built on NGMPP images (155 pages, 3,100 lines) says "the dataset we used for evaluation is confidential". — [arXiv 2512.17111](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17111v1)

**eGangotri / archive.org**
- eGangotri ("eGangotri Digital Preservation Trust – India") posts scans to archive.org. A July 2025 post claims "62,000 Rare books and manuscripts digitized as of Nov. 2024"; a critic in the same forum counted 24,219 PDFs across 16 accounts. These figures are disputed. — [bvparishat thread](https://groups.google.com/g/bvparishat/c/KkqRwrJa-u8/m/gv4yiP5xBAAJ) (seen via a search snippet only); [eGangotri GitHub](https://github.com/eGangotri)
- The Kashmir Research Institute collection on archive.org has 1,751 items (my count). — [kashmirresearchinstitute](https://archive.org/details/kashmirresearchinstitute)
- eGangotri itself publishes images only. Besides the Muktabodha e-texts above, I found no systematic set of transcriptions keyed to eGangotri scans.

**National Mission for Manuscripts / Gyan Bharatam / IGNCA**
- The NMM set up "Kritisampada, the National Database of Manuscripts", a bibliographic database. — [Wikipedia: NMM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Mission_for_Manuscripts). From here, namami.gov.in and bharatiyakritisampada.nic.in did not connect on 27 Nov 2026.
- The NMM has been restructured as the "Gyan Bharatam Mission" (2024–31). A government release says "more than 4.40 lakh manuscripts are available on Gyan Bharatam portal at present for public access, in view mode", with restrictions possible for sensitive material. — [PIB release 2293807](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2293807) (403 to my fetcher; quoted from a search snippet). A Feb 2026 report gave over 7.5 lakh digitised, with 1.29 lakh on the portal. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/over-7-5-lakh-manuscripts-digitised-under-gyan-bharatam-45-centres-onboarded-govt-to-ls/2843844). The portal [gyanbharatam.gov.in](https://gyanbharatam.gov.in/) resolves (200), but it is a JavaScript single-page app with a "devtools-guard" script, and I could not inspect it for transcriptions.
- IGNCA: Kalāsampadā/Kalānidhi hold microfilmed manuscripts. Consultation is free in the reference library, and copies are "provided in digital, microfilm or print formats for a fee", subject to MOUs with the holding libraries. The Vidya Nidhi repository says items are protected by copyright, "all rights reserved by IGNCA unless stated otherwise". — [IGNCA microfilm collection](https://ignca.gov.in/?p=38596); [IGNCA reference library access](https://ignca.gov.in/access-to-reference-library-resources/); [Vidya Nidhi](https://ignca.gov.in/?p=114720). I found no transcriptions.
- **AnciDev** (IIT Mandi / BharatGen) uses NMM archive pages: "3,000 transcribed text lines from 500 pages of different ancient Devanagari manuscripts" in Hindi and Sanskrit, 16th–19th c. Data is on [Google Drive](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1wOqHgSUPieIoBwQ1ACn6ixpOxBuVo3T6?usp=sharing). The GitHub API reports `license: null`. — [AnciDev GitHub](https://github.com/vriti2003/AnciDev); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.bhasha-1.8/)

**Jain sources**
- **Kobā (Ācārya Śrī Kailāsasāgarasūri Jñānamandira):** "about 2,50,000 manuscripts", "about 3,000 manuscripts written on the palm leaves". Scholars "have been facilitated with the arrangement of obtaining the photocopies of manuscripts". There are no online images or transcriptions; the "Terms & Condition" link on the home page is a placeholder. — [Kobā manuscripts section](https://www.kobatirth.org/gyan-tirth/gyanmandir/manuscripts/) (verified); [kobatirth.org](https://kobatirth.org/)
- The Kobā catalogue *Kailāsa Śrutasāgara Granthasūcī* offers look-ups "आदिवाक्य से कृति माहिती" and "अंतिमवाक्य से कृति माहिती" (work by opening sentence and by closing sentence), and notes "कम्प्यूटर पर ये सूचनाएँ और भी विस्तार से उपलब्ध हैं" (the computer version has more detail). The full field description is in vol. 1.1.1, pp. 33–38. — [Kailas Shrutasagar Granthsuchi vol. 2 (2004), p. 15](https://jainqq.org/explore/018025/15)
- **L. D. Institute:** the printed *Catalogue of Sanskrit and Prakrit Manuscripts* (Muni Puṇyavijaya collection; Part 2, L. D. Indology, 1965) gives "Beg." lines and colophon notes for some entries, readable as OCR on jainqq.org. — [jainqq.org, Part 2](https://jainqq.org/explore/018007/168). The Rajasthan Oriental Research Institute, Jodhpur catalogue (Jinvijay, 1963) gives mostly metadata and only occasional opening lines. — [jainqq.org](https://jainqq.org/explore/018011/226)
- **JAINpedia (Institute of Jainology):** manuscript pages show folio images with metadata. Example: British Library Or. 2120 ms. B, *Śrāddhavidhi-viniścaya*, Sanskrit and Prakrit, 15th–16th c., western India.
  - The terms on the page say "Copyright: CC0 1.0 (Creative Commons Public Domain)" and offer "Download image". The footer says "© 2026 JAINPEDIA, All Rights Reserved".
  - The page has a "Transcription" tab, but in the HTML I downloaded its panel is empty.
  - Source: [JAINpedia, Section 1](https://jainpedia.org/manuscript/section-1)
  - The Institute's newer digitisation plan lists "Translation, transliteration, audio" as intended features, but names no digitised Indian collections. — [Institute of Jainology: Digitisation & Contextualisation](https://www.jainology.org/digitisation-contextualisation/)
- **Jain journals that edit short works from manuscripts:** Kobā's monthly *Śrutasāgara* (PDFs, e.g. [Jan 2022](https://www.jainfoundation.in/JAINLIBRARY/books/shrutsagar_2022_01_volume_08_08_525377_data.pdf)) and *Anusandhāna* ([1996 no. 7 PDF](https://www.jainfoundation.in/JAINLIBRARY/books/Anusandhan_1996_00_SrNo_07_520507_HR.pdf)) are on the Jain eLibrary / jainfoundation.in mirrors. I did not find a feature that systematically prints manuscript facsimiles with line-for-line transcriptions. The one *Anusandhāna* page I read (1997 no. 10, p. 94) is an article on a Gujarati rāsa edited "on the basis of a manuscript in the L. D. Institute of Indology", with no facsimile. — [jainqq.org](https://jainqq.org/explore/520510/94)
- **Jaina Prosopography** (SOAS, with Kobā and Jain Vishva Bharati, Ladnun) is an open-access database built from colophon data. Its page describes outputs in the future tense and gives no licence. — [SOAS project page](https://www.soas.ac.uk/research/jaina-prosopography-monastic-lineages-networks-and-patronage); [jaina-prosopography.org](https://jaina-prosopography.org/) (verified, 200)
- The 45 Śvetāmbara Āgama texts are available as Unicode Devanagari files through the Jain eLibrary. These are editions, not manuscript transcriptions. — [Indology list, Jun 2018](https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/2018-June/047815.html) (search snippet)

**Sanskrit Library (US; named in the brief)**
- An NEH project (PR-50178-13, 2013–15) built "human-validated automated text-image alignment techniques" on "the 160-manuscript, 25,000-image prototype … of Sanskrit manuscripts" at Brown and Penn. It had IIT Bombay participants (Malhar Kulkarni, Anuja and Tanuja Ajotikar). The project page gives no URLs or licence for the transcriptions. — [Sanskrit Library: NEH 2012 project](https://sanskritlibrary.org/neh2012dev.html)
- The final report (2016) says the "Transcribed data included initially 7 pages of UPenn 2173, and then 1,063 pages". — [core.ac.uk PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/147825347.pdf) (blocked to me; quoted from a search snippet)
- The Sanskrit Library's "ManuscriptTranscription" tool "Implements a floating text area that can move over the image" and was "Used to generate training data for an OCR program". No dataset is offered. — [ManuscriptTranscription](https://sanskritlibrary.org/software/manuscriptTranscription.html)

**Oriental Research Institutes and university projects in India**
- **BORI Digital Library** is subscription-based: "annual pack individual subscription or … corporate subscription", "$50–$600 per year", with a limited free pass. — [CRL eDesiderata review](https://edesiderata.crl.edu/resources/bhandarkar-oriental-research-institute-bori-digital-library). borilib.com did not connect from here. I found no transcriptions.
- **Lalchand Research Library, DAV College, Chandigarh** ([dav.splrarebooks.com](https://dav.splrarebooks.com/)): 6,037 Devanagari Sanskrit manuscripts online, high-resolution and viewable. Terms: "Viewers are not to sell, alter or further reproduce or distribute any part of the Manuscripts. Failure to comply … will invite legal action". Images only.
- **FLAME University, Pune** built layout ground truth on these Lalchand images: "15 Sanskrit Manuscripts, 481 pages", in PAGE-XML.
  - The released XML that I checked has polygons and no `<Unicode>` text.
  - The images are not redistributed: "Original images are not provided with the dataset due to copyright reasons". The dataset ships URLs instead, with a fair-use disclaimer stating the images are "© Lalchand Research Library".
  - Source: [gnn-synthetic-layout-historical, gram-submission branch](https://github.com/flame-cai/gnn-synthetic-layout-historical/tree/gram-submission) (code GPL-3.0)
  - Their 2026 paper says three manuscripts carry "Unicode annotations in the standard PAGE-XML format". — [arXiv 2608.18696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18696)
  - Their earlier HTR study (WSC 2025) targets a Lalchand writing style. — [ACL Anthology 2025.wsc-csdh.4](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wsc-csdh.4.pdf) (too large for my fetcher)
- **Vrindavan Research Institute:** SOAS hosts a 1978 photographic record ("From the Land of Braj"). I found no VRI portal and no transcriptions. — [SOAS BRAJ collection](https://digital.soas.ac.uk/BRAJ)
- **Pracalit side note (Nepal, not Devanagari):** ground truth from four Sanskrit/Newar manuscripts in Pracalit script, 441 training and 242 validation pages, CC BY 4.0. — [Zenodo 6967421](https://zenodo.org/record/6967421); [JOHD paper](https://soas-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/419512)

- Ambuda: "All of our proofing projects are based on scans of physical books", i.e. printed public-domain books, not manuscripts. — [Ambuda proofing help](https://ambuda.org/proofing/help/complete-guide) (seen via a search snippet; the page did not render to my fetcher)

### Inferences
- The best ready-made line-level ground truth is the set of 8 Muktabodha diplomatic e-texts whose eGangotri scans are still live: about 1,630 folio-side markers by my count, mostly Jammu Raghunath Temple and UPSS manuscripts. The text is scriptio continua with lines already broken where the manuscript breaks them, which fits a CTC line recogniser well.
  - The hand is Kashmiri/North-Indian Brahmanical, not Jain Nāgarī. These texts suit a general Devanagari base model better than the Jain-specific fine-tune.
  - The other diplomatic e-texts are only useful if their images are recovered, from Muktabodha, the Dharmartha Trust or older archive.org derivatives.
- The IFP transcripts give a very large supply of 20th-century handwritten Devanagari (210,000 pages) with matching page-numbered e-texts for about 69 titles. That makes them a cheap source of extra line data. But the hand is a clean modern pandit hand, and the e-texts add word spaces, so the spacing must be removed or modelled before training. The ND licence on the images may limit redistributing line crops, though not necessarily internal training.
- NGMCP excerpts give only a few lines per manuscript, in IAST, and must be converted to Devanagari. Their real value is as a check on opening and closing lines. With the brief's 15th–19th c. paper Devanagari focus, about 48% of entries fit.
- For the requester's own Jain material, the most usable "existing transcriptions" are the opening- and closing-sentence fields in the Kobā and L. D. Institute catalogues for the very manuscripts they hold. These are partial, a few lines per manuscript, but they come from the same manuscripts, so they could seed first and last lines without fresh typing.
- None of the sources found addresses machine-learning training explicitly. CC BY-NC 4.0 (Muktabodha e-texts) and CC0 (most surviving eGangotri items, JAINpedia images) are the most permissive terms found. ND, "no reproduce" and "all rights reserved" terms (IFP images, Lalchand, IGNCA) are the main constraints.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether the Muktabodha e-texts added after Nov 2024 (the library says 570+ e-texts; the zip has 499) include more diplomatic manuscript transcriptions. The new interface, muktabodha-digital-library.org, was not crawled.
- For the darked eGangotri items, I did not establish whether the images survive elsewhere (Muktabodha's own copies, the Dharmartha Trust, Gyan Bharatam). The script of each of the 44 source manuscripts was not checked one by one; MS 3887 at least is Śāradā.
- I could not inspect Gyan Bharatam, Kritisampada/Namami, the Indian Culture portal (indianculture.gov.in/manuscripts) or jainelibrary.org: they are JavaScript-only or unreachable. Whether Gyan Bharatam publishes any transcriptions or HTR output is unknown.
- I found no evidence that Jain Vishva Bharati (Ladnun), Shrut Ratnakar (Ahmedabad), the L. D. Institute or Kobā publish manuscript images online with transcriptions. Shrut Ratnakar's own publications were not found.
- AnciDev's per-page provenance (which NMM repositories), transcription conventions and licence terms are not stated in the README. The NMM's permission for redistribution is unknown.
- IIT Kanpur, CDAC and the Sanskrit universities: I found no project releasing transcribed handwritten Devanagari Sanskrit manuscripts with images.
- ORI Mysore: I found nothing on transcriptions; its holdings are mostly non-Devanagari scripts in any case (not verified).

## Key Question 2: For each source, what is available, in which script, how faithful is the transcription, how are images obtained, and on what terms?

### Takeaway
Sources whose transcriptions follow the manuscript, with folio or line breaks: Muktabodha eGangotri-based diplomatic e-texts (full text), Muktabodha IFP-transcript e-texts (full text, page-marked, word-spaced) and NGMCP (excerpts only). All other South Asian sources offer only images, only metadata, or only editions. Terms run from CC0 (surviving eGangotri items, JAINpedia images) through CC BY-NC 4.0 (Muktabodha e-texts) and CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 (IFP images) to paid or all-rights-reserved (BORI, IGNCA, Lalchand).

### Inferences
- The Muktabodha header itself states the manuscript number and, for some texts, the archive.org link. So pairing is deterministic wherever the image survives, but each e-text still needs a folio-by-folio check. Revision 0 texts were "not normalized", while later revisions were made diplomatic, so the latest revision should be used.
- A CC BY-NC 4.0 e-text paired with a CC0 image allows non-commercial training and redistribution of line pairs with attribution. That fits the requester's CC BY-SA 4.0 release plan only if NC material is kept apart, because BY-NC content cannot be relicensed as BY-SA.

### Gaps
- No source states terms specifically for machine-learning training.
- The licence of the NGMCP wiki text is not stated on the mirror or the archive page.
- The Kobā photocopy and scan terms are not published online.

## Key Question 3: Are there freely available printed facsimile editions that reproduce a Devanagari Sanskrit (or Jain) manuscript together with a line-by-line transcription?

### Takeaway
I found none that is freely available and meets the criterion. Searches turned up only manuscript facsimiles without transcriptions, or editions without facsimiles. The closest usable "edition that follows one manuscript closely" category is Muktabodha's diplomatic e-texts (Question 1), not a printed book.

### Cited Findings
- The Bodleian holds a full digital facsimile of a Jambūdvīpaprajñapti paper manuscript in Jaina Nāgarī. It is a facsimile only, with no transcription. — [Bodleian (marco.ox.ac.uk)](https://marco.ox.ac.uk/ark:29072/x0cr56n143dw) (search result; outside South Asia)
- Hoernle's *Uvāsagadasāo* (Bibliotheca Indica, 1890) prints the text with commentary and ṭabbā from manuscripts but is a critical edition, not a facsimile. — [archive.org PDF](https://ia800200.us.archive.org/34/items/Bibliotheca_Indica_Series/UvasagadasaoEnglishTranslation-AfRudolfHoernle1890bis_text.pdf) (search snippet)
- A 2024 *International Journal of Jaina Studies* article on Vāgbhaṭa's *Chandonuśāsana* describes two manuscripts (Patan, and a 1939/40 copy that reproduces the first one's errors) ahead of an edition. — [SOAS eprints](https://eprints.soas.ac.uk/43278/1/Chondonushasana%20of%20Vagbhata%20FINAL.pdf) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- Single-manuscript editions in *Śrutasāgara* and *Anusandhāna*, and Shodhganga editions, are the likeliest places to find a printed text that follows one Jain manuscript closely. Each would have to be checked by hand for a reproduced folio and the base manuscript's identity.

### Gaps
- I did not find a catalogue of facsimile editions (e.g. a L. D. Series, Patan Hemacandrācārya Jñānamandira or Sarabhai Nawab volume) that pairs plates with a line-for-line transcription. This needs a targeted bibliographic search of the Jain eLibrary, which I could not query because the site is JavaScript-only.
